You are an experienced image-quality analyst, data scientist and NGO brand strategist.
We have a dataset of 4,000 photographs:
- 3,000 historically approved.
- 1,000 historically rejected.
Every photograph has been independently evaluated using the same NGO-specific visual evaluation rubric.
The annotations include:
- Individual assessments against 16 NGO rules.
- Technical quality tags.
- Composition tags.
- Editorial and storytelling tags.
- Brand-related tags.
- Privacy and safeguarding flags.
- Contextual tags.
- Model-generated potential rejection factors.

You will receive aggregated statistics comparing the approved and rejected groups:
{PROGRAMMATIC_STATISTICS_JSON}

Your task is to identify patterns that can improve our future photo approval engine.

Important distinction:
The historical label tells you what decision the NGO made.
It does not tell you why that decision was made.
The Gemini-generated tags are model observations, not confirmed explanations for historical decisions.
Do not treat correlations as confirmed causes.

Analyse the following:
1. Identify the most prominent differences between approved and rejected photographs.
2. Identify factors that are disproportionately represented in rejected photographs.
3. Identify factors that are common in both categories and therefore may not discriminate well.
4. Identify factors that occur frequently in approved photographs but should still be retained as important policy or safety checks.
5. Identify combinations of tags that appear frequently in rejected photographs.
6. Identify rejection patterns that remain unexplained by the existing 16 rules.
7. Identify any potential weaknesses in the current evaluation taxonomy.
8. Identify possible event-specific or photography-session-related biases.
9. Recommend additional evaluation criteria for the enhanced photo assessment rubric.
10. Recommend which discovered criteria should be mandatory, advisory or subject to human review.

Output:
Return valid JSON containing:
A. `key_findings`: A ranked list of patterns supported by the supplied statistics.
B. `additional_evaluation_factors`: For each factor, include `proposed_name`, `description`, `supporting_evidence`, `approved_prevalence`, `rejected_prevalence`, `prevalence_difference`, `visually_observable`, `external_verification_required`, and `recommended_enforcement` (MANDATORY | ADVISORY | HUMAN_REVIEW).
C. `rulebook_gaps`: Considerations not adequately captured by the existing 16 rules.
D. `potential_false_assumptions`: Factors that might appear important but are not sufficiently supported by the dataset.
E. `enhanced_evaluation_rubric`: A proposed rubric combining the original 16 NGO rules, validated additional technical criteria, editorial and storytelling criteria, brand suitability criteria, and compliance, privacy and safeguarding considerations.

Do not invent policy requirements or claim that any factor is an actual historical rejection reason without human confirmation.
Make recommendations based on evidence, clearly identifying uncertainty and limitations.
