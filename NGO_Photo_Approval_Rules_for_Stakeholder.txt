NGO PHOTO APPROVAL: THE RULES THE AI IS USING
Prepared for stakeholder review, 6 November 2026
Rule set: layered_final_v1 (demo/test version, not yet approved by the NGO)
Prepared by: Dhiren Ganwani (Roll no 27PGAI0007)

1. HOW A PHOTO IS JUDGED
========================

A vision AI (Claude Haiku 5.5) looks at one photograph and answers a fixed list of yes/no questions
about it, with a short reason for each answer. It also grades six quality areas from 0 to 4. The AI
does NOT decide. Fixed program rules (not the AI) turn its answers into the verdict, so the same
answers always give the same verdict.

The verdict is always Approve or Disapprove, with the reasons. A person still reviews every
photograph. Nothing is published automatically. Reviewer feedback is used to improve the rules (see
section 8).

A photograph is APPROVED only when ALL of these hold:
  -  every mandatory check in section 3 is answered YES (or NOT APPLICABLE where the photo does not
     contain the thing asked about);
  -  the overall quality score is at least 65 out of 100 (section 5);
  -  no quality area is graded 0 (unusable) or 1 (poor);
  -  no publication blocker is found (section 6): a blocked consent or rights status, or a visual
     flag that blocks publication. Checks that are only still waiting to be confirmed do not stop
     approval; they must be confirmed by people before publishing.

Anything else is DISAPPROVED. If the AI cannot tell (for example a face is too small to judge), the
answer counts as not confirmed, so the photo is not approved. The aim is to avoid false approvals
first.

2. THE NGO'S OWN RULES, AND HOW THEY ARE GIVEN TO THE AI
========================================================

The NGO's document has 16 rules and marks none of them as mandatory. The wording below is the NGO's
original. Where the AI is given different wording (the rule was reworded after the owner's review of
5-6 November 2026), the AI's wording is shown beneath it.

R001  Banner Visibility & Presentation
    NGO's wording: If a banner is present, ensure it is clearly visible, properly positioned,
    straight and free from folds, crumpling or creases.
    Given to the AI as: If a banner is present, is at least 30% of it visible and readable? Blocking
    by people and minor creases are fine.

R002  Volunteer Dress Code
    NGO's wording: Volunteers should wear white-on-white or a white top with jeans. Ensure clothing
    is neat, clean and presentable.
    Given to the AI as: Volunteers giving something to a beneficiary should wear white tops.

R003  Beneficiary Visibility
    NGO's wording: Ensure the beneficiary’s face and expressions are clearly visible, wherever
    appropriate. Avoid obstructed faces and awkward cropping.
    Given to the AI as written.

R004  Natural Expressions
    NGO's wording: Capture genuine expressions and interactions. Beneficiaries and volunteers should
    not look straight into the camera unless a specific photograph requires it.
    Given to the AI as: Capture natural expressions and interactions. A posed photograph, such as a
    group photograph of volunteers, is fine.

R005  Natural & Candid Shots
    NGO's wording: Prioritise candid moments that capture compassion, kindness, shots that show
    genuine interaction with beneficiaries rather than staged or overly posed photographs.
    Not given to the AI as a separate rule.

R006  Capture the Actual Seva
    NGO's wording: Clearly showcase the activity being performed, whether food distribution,
    education support, healthcare assistance, animal care or another seva initiative.
    Given to the AI as written.

R007  Proper Camera Angle
    NGO's wording: Choose an appropriate angle that captures the activity, beneficiary and volunteer
    interaction clearly. Avoid awkward perspectives, tilted frames and angles that obstruct the
    subject.
    Given to the AI as: Choose an angle that captures the activity from the same level. Avoid
    awkward perspectives, tilted frames and angles that obstruct the subject. A very high or very
    low angle is not allowed; a slightly higher or lower angle is fine.

R008  Volunteer Positioning
    NGO's wording: Avoid photographs in which the volunteer’s back is prominently visible. Prefer
    front or side angles that highlight the volunteer’s involvement in the activity.
    Given to the AI as: Show people from the front or the side. Nobody should be shown fully from
    behind: some part of the face should be visible.

R009  Proper Lighting
    NGO's wording: Ensure subjects are well-lit and clearly visible. Avoid dark, overexposed,
    harshly backlit or shadow-heavy photographs.
    Given to the AI as written.

R010  Focus & Image Clarity
    NGO's wording: Ensure photographs are sharp and in focus. Avoid blurry, grainy or shaky images.
    Given to the AI as written.

R011  Clean Background
    NGO's wording: Avoid distracting backgrounds, clutter, scattered materials and unrelated objects
    that take attention away from the seva.
    Given to the AI as written.

R012  Final QC Test Q4
    NGO's wording: Does it maintain the dignity of everyone involved?
    Not given to the AI as a separate rule. It is still checked through: R012_DIGNITY.

R013  Final QC Test Q5
    NGO's wording: Does it reflect the spirit of humanity and compassion?
    Not given to the AI as a separate rule. It is still checked through: R013_HUMANITY.

R014  Final QC Test Q1
    NGO's wording: Is the seva activity clearly visible?
    Not given to the AI as a separate rule.

R015  Final QC Test Q2
    NGO's wording: Are the volunteer and beneficiary captured appropriately?
    Not given to the AI as a separate rule. It is still checked through: R015_PERSON_PRESENT,
    R015_BEN_RECEIVING.

R016  Final QC Test Q3
    NGO's wording: Is the photograph natural, well-framed and properly lit?
    Not given to the AI as a separate rule.


3. MANDATORY CHECKS (a NO or 'cannot tell' means Disapprove)
============================================================

These are the NGO team's policy, applied on top of the NGO's document. Each is a question the AI
answers about the photo.

Gate G001  [R007_ANGLE]  (broken down from NGO rule R007; policy reference 1)
    Is the activity captured from roughly the same level as the people (a slightly higher or lower
    angle is fine, a very high or very low angle is not), with a level frame, so that the activity
    is clearly visible and not obstructed?

Gate G003  [R006_ITEM_SHARP]  (NGO team rule; policy reference 2)
    If an item is being handed over, is the item sharp and in focus and clear enough to recognise? A
    sack or similar packed goods count as items, and their contents need not be visible. Text,
    labels or print on the item (for example the words on newspapers) do not need to be readable:
    answer YES when you can tell what kind of item it is.

Gate G004  [R003_FACE]  (broken down from NGO rule R003; policy reference 3)
    Is some part of every person's face visible, even if turned partly away: not covered, hidden or
    in shadow, and, for the main people, not too small to read?

Gate G006  [R003_FACE_SHARP]  (NGO team rule; policy reference 5)
    Is every person's face sharp and in focus (not blurred by motion or poor focus)?

Gate G009  [R001_FLAT]  (broken down from NGO rule R001; policy reference 4)
    Is the banner free from crumpling so severe that its words cannot be read? Ordinary folds,
    creases and soft ripples are fine.

Gate G012  [R015_BEN_RECEIVING]  (NGO team rule; policy reference 7)
    If an item is being given, is at least one beneficiary receiving it (or already holding it)?

Gate G013  [R002_GIVER_WHITE]  (NGO team rule; policy reference 7)
    Is every person who is giving something to a beneficiary wearing a white top?

Gate G014  [R015_PERSON_PRESENT]  (NGO team rule; policy reference 8)
    Is at least one relevant subject visible: a beneficiary, a volunteer, or an animal receiving
    care?

Gate G016  [R008_BACK]  (broken down from NGO rule R008; policy reference NGO-2026-11-02-5)
    Is every person seen from the front or the side, with some part of the face visible (nobody
    shown fully from behind)?

Gate G017  [R001_VISIBLE]  (broken down from NGO rule R001; policy reference NGO-2026-11-02-2)
    If a banner is present, is at least 30% of it visible and readable? Blocking by people and minor
    creases are fine.

Gate G019  [R001_POSITION]  (broken down from NGO rule R001; policy reference OWNER-2026-11-05-1)
    Is the entire banner, judged by its assumed boundary even where people or objects hide part of
    it, within the frame of the photo?


4. OTHER CHECKS (they feed the quality score; they do not reject by themselves)
===============================================================================

[R003_CROP]  (broken down from NGO rule R003; counts towards: Activity and subject communication)
    Is every beneficiary fully inside the frame, with no face or body cut off by the frame edge?

[R003_OBSTRUCTION_PPE_ONLY]  (NGO team rule; counts towards: Activity and subject communication)
    If any beneficiary's face is obstructed, is the ONLY obstruction necessary medical protective
    equipment (mask, face shield)?

[R004_GENUINE]  (broken down from NGO rule R004; counts towards: Naturalness and storytelling)
    Do the expressions and interactions look natural, with nothing forced or uncomfortable? A posed
    photograph is fine, including a posed group photograph of volunteers, and answers YES.

[R006_ACTION]  (broken down from NGO rule R006; counts towards: Activity and subject communication)
    Does the photograph clearly show a seva activity being performed, such as giving food, teaching,
    treating, caring for an animal, or a beneficiary receiving assistance, rather than only people
    standing or waiting?

[R007_CUTOFF]  (NGO team rule; counts towards: Composition and framing)
    Is everything important fully inside the frame: no key person, hands or objects cut off at the
    edges or hidden by the camera angle?

[R007_DISTORTION]  (broken down from NGO rule R007; counts towards: Composition and framing)
    Is the photo free from obvious wide-angle distortion (stretched or bent faces, bowed edges)?

[R007_PLACEMENT]  (NGO team rule; counts towards: Composition and framing)
    Is the main subject sensibly placed in the frame (not squeezed into a corner or edge) and free
    from obstruction by people or objects in front of it?

[R011_BACKGROUND]  (broken down from NGO rule R011; counts towards: Visual polish)
    Is the background free from clutter, scattered materials and unrelated objects that draw
    attention away from the seva?

[R012_DIGNITY]  (broken down from NGO rule R012; counts towards: Naturalness and storytelling)
    Does the photograph maintain the dignity of everyone involved?

[R013_HUMANITY]  (broken down from NGO rule R013; counts towards: Naturalness and storytelling)
    Does the photograph reflect the spirit of humanity and compassion?

[NEW-014]  (proposed, awaiting NGO approval; counts towards: Visual polish)
    Is the photograph free from unintended watermark, editing marks or camera overlays?

Two more checks (R006_SIZE and R010_TECHNICAL) are 'soft mandatory': they do not reject by
themselves, but if the AI answers NO to them the related quality area is capped at grade 2
(acceptable with visible flaws), which pulls the score down:
  -  R006_SIZE NO -> Activity and subject communication is at most 2 (main people or the activity
     are small, far or blocked).
  -  R010_TECHNICAL NO -> Technical quality is at most 2 (focus, blur, exposure or lighting
     problem).

R003_OBSTRUCTION_PPE_ONLY is only used together with the medical exception in section 7.

5. QUALITY SCORE
================

The AI grades five areas from 0 (unusable) to 4 (excellent). The program combines them using these
weights into a score out of 100. An area that does not apply is left out of the calculation. Brand
presentation is NOT graded or counted at all (owner decision of 6 November 2026: too subjective), so
the weights below add up to 100 on their own.

  27.8%  Activity and subject communication
  22.2%  Technical quality
  22.2%  Composition and framing
  16.7%  Naturalness and storytelling
  11.1%  Visual polish

Grades: 0 = Unusable, 1 = Poor, 2 = Acceptable, 3 = Good, 4 = Excellent.

Cutoff: a score below 65 out of 100 means Disapprove (re-select). The cutoff was lowered from 70 to
65 on 6 November 2026 at the owner's request. A grade of 0 or 1 in any area also means Disapprove,
whatever the total, so a photo can be disapproved with a total above the cutoff; the screen then
names the poor area. For every area graded 2 or lower the AI must give a short reason, which is
shown on the review screen.

Extra guidance the AI is given for two areas:
  -  Technical quality: 0 = unusable (almost black, blown out or too blurred to make out the
     activity); 1 = poor (a serious problem with focus, exposure or lighting); 2 = acceptable with
     visible flaws; 3 = good; 4 = excellent. Judge focus, blur, grain, exposure and lighting
     together here, and count each defect only once.
  -  Activity and subject communication: 0 = the main people and the activity cannot be made out at
     all; 1 = barely visible (tiny, far away or hidden); 2 = visible but small or partly blocked; 3
     = clear; 4 = excellent. A posed group photograph of volunteers, with the banner or the items
     they give, is clear: do not lower this grade because no seva action is under way or no
     beneficiary is shown.
  -  Naturalness and storytelling: A posed photograph is fine, including a posed group photograph of
     volunteers: never lower this grade because the photo is posed or shows only volunteers. Grade 1
     or lower only for a scene that looks forced, uncomfortable or undignified; 2 = ordinary but
     flat; 3 = natural; 4 = excellent.

6. PUBLICATION CHECKS (things that cannot be seen in a photograph)
==================================================================

These cannot be seen in a photograph, so the AI never claims they are verified. People confirm them
before publishing. While they are outstanding the photo can still be approved, marked as waiting for
these checks; a status of blocked disapproves it:
  -  Consent and publication permission
  -  Copyright and image usage rights
  -  Child safeguarding verification
  -  Caption accurately describes the photograph
  -  Event details and claims verified
  -  Credits and usage rights documented

The AI also reports visual flags if it sees them: confidential information, suspected manipulation,
unsafe activity, degrading representation, a possible minor (needs verification, not a statement
about age), third-party branding, sensitive location or situation. The first four block publication.

7. EXCEPTIONS
=============

    In a medical scene, a face covered only by necessary protective equipment (mask, face shield) is
    not automatically a violation. It is not waved through either: the rule engine treats it as
    undecided.


8. HOW REVIEWER FEEDBACK CHANGES THE RULES
==========================================

When a reviewer disagrees with the AI, a second AI analyses the reasons. It may propose a rewording
of the specific rule the reviewer contradicted. A proposal is applied automatically only if safety
checks pass (for example, a YES answer must still mean the requirement is met). Changes to rules
from the NGO's own document are flagged for the developers. Every proposal and its outcome is
recorded.

Changes currently live in the demo from reviewer feedback:
  -  R006_ACTION (applied 2026-11-06 08:17):
        Now reads: Does the photograph clearly show a seva activity being performed, such as giving
        food, teaching, treating, caring for an animal, or a beneficiary receiving assistance,
        rather than only people standing or waiting?

Owner decision of 6 November 2026, written into the rules by hand (the automatic rewrite of
R004_GENUINE had been rejected by the safety check): a posed photograph is never disapproved for
being posed, including a posed group photograph of volunteers with no beneficiary. R004_GENUINE now
answers YES for a posed photo, the client line R004 says so, and the quality guidance for
Naturalness and Activity tells the AI not to lower those grades for a posed or volunteer-only photo.
